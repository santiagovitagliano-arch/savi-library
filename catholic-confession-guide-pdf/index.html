Reassurance Before You Begin

The priest has heard every possible version of every possible sin. There is nothing you can bring that has not been brought before. He is not there to judge you. He is there to extend the mercy of Christ. The whole reason for his vocation is to do exactly what you are about to ask him to do. The doors really do open in.

I. Brief Examination of Conscience

The First Three Commandments — God

  • Have I neglected daily prayer?
  • Have I placed money, work, image, or relationships before God?
  • Have I used God's name in vain — including casual exclamations?
  • Have I missed Mass on Sunday through my own fault?
  • Have I been irreverent at Mass, or received Communion in a state of grave sin?

Commandments 4-5 — Neighbor (Family and Life)

  • Have I disrespected my parents in word, tone, or action? Have I neglected aging parents who need me?
  • Have I failed to instruct, discipline, or be present to my children?
  • Have I held anger, hatred, or grudges?
  • Have I participated in or supported abortion, euthanasia, or contraception?
  • Have I harmed my own body through excess, recklessness, or self-neglect?
  • Have I been violent in word — cruel, sarcastic, publicly humiliating?
  • Have I refused to forgive someone who has asked? Refused to seek forgiveness from someone I have wronged?

Commandments 6 & 9 — Purity

  • Have I engaged in sexual activity outside marriage?
  • Have I committed adultery, including emotional adultery?
  • Have I used pornography or masturbated?
  • Have I entertained impure thoughts willingly?
  • Have I dressed or behaved to provoke lust in others?

Commandments 7 & 10 — Property and Honesty

  • Have I taken what does not belong to me — including time at work, software, small office items?
  • Have I cheated on taxes, tests, or expense reports?
  • Have I failed to pay debts I could have paid?
  • Have I envied another's success, possessions, or relationships?
  • Have I been greedy?
  • Have I given to the poor in proportion to what I have been given?

Commandment 8 — Truth

  • Have I lied, including by exaggeration, omission, or silence?
  • Have I gossiped or spread rumors?
  • Have I damaged another person's reputation?
  • Have I broken a confidence?
  • Have I judged others harshly in my heart?

Sins of Omission

  • The good I should have done and did not
  • The person who needed me whom I avoided
  • The wrong I witnessed and did not name
  • The talents I have not used for any purpose worthy of them

For the longer treatment of each commandment with more concrete examples, see our companion guide on examples of sins to say at confession.

II. The Seven Steps of Confession

  1. Enter and greet. Make the Sign of the Cross. Wait for the priest.
  2. Open: "Bless me, Father, for I have sinned. It has been [time] since my last confession."
  3. Confess your sins by kind. For grave matter, give a rough number. Do not narrate.
  4. Listen to the priest's counsel and receive your penance.
  5. Pray the Act of Contrition (see prayers below).
  6. Receive absolution. Make the Sign of the Cross.
  7. Leave and perform your penance as soon as is practical.

For the full step-by-step walkthrough with what to do if you forget the prayers, see how to go to confession.

III. The Prayers

The Act of Contrition (Traditional)

O my God, I am heartily sorry for having offended Thee, and I detest all my sins because of Thy just punishments, but most of all because they offend Thee, my God, who art all good and deserving of all my love. I firmly resolve, with the help of Thy grace, to sin no more and to avoid the near occasions of sin. Amen.

The Act of Contrition (Modern, from the Rite of Penance)

My God, I am sorry for my sins with all my heart. In choosing to do wrong and failing to do good, I have sinned against You whom I should love above all things. I firmly intend, with Your help, to do penance, to sin no more, and to avoid whatever leads me to sin. Our Savior Jesus Christ suffered and died for us. In His name, my God, have mercy. Amen.

The Act of Contrition (Short Form)

O my God, I am sorry for my sins. I love You with all my heart. I am sorry I have hurt You. Help me to live like Jesus and not sin again. Amen.

For the full reading on the prayer — its meaning line by line, when to pray it, and what to do if you forget — see the Act of Contrition.

IV. After You Leave

  • Perform your penance as soon as is practical, ideally before the day ends.
  • Make a brief act of thanksgiving — a short prayer in the pew or quietly on the walk home.
  • Go to Mass. Receive Communion. The grace of the sacrament flows through the rest of the sacramental life.
  • Resolve to return regularly. Monthly is the recommended rhythm. Annual at minimum.
