Examples of Sins to Say at Confession

A complete examination of conscience, organized by the Commandments.

If you are preparing for confession and do not know where to begin, this examination of conscience is for you. It is organized by the Ten Commandments and the seven capital sins, with concrete examples of what each kind of sin actually looks like in modern life — and how to name it clearly when you are kneeling at the screen or sitting face to face with the priest.

A note before you begin: this list is comprehensive, not exhaustive. Read through it slowly. The Holy Spirit will surface what needs to be brought. You do not need to find something for every category, and you should not strain to invent sins. Confess what is real, name it briefly and clearly, and trust the sacrament.

I. Examination by the Ten Commandments

1st Commandment — I am the Lord your God; you shall not have other gods before me.

  • Have I neglected daily prayer?
  • Have I treated success, money, status, work, relationships, or my own image as more important than God?
  • Have I consulted horoscopes, tarot, mediums, or other occult practices?
  • Have I doubted the faith publicly when speaking would have been right, or claimed to believe what I do not?
  • Have I despaired of God's mercy, or presumed upon it without conversion?

2nd Commandment — You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain.

  • Have I used God's name, Jesus' name, or Mary's name as an exclamation or in anger?
  • Have I cursed others, or wished evil on someone, even in passing?
  • Have I broken a vow, oath, or serious promise made in God's name?
  • Have I spoken about sacred things — the Eucharist, the saints, the Scriptures — with contempt or mockery?

3rd Commandment — Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy.

  • Have I missed Sunday Mass through my own fault?
  • Have I been distracted, late, or irreverent during Mass?
  • Have I done unnecessary servile work on Sunday, or treated the day as ordinary?
  • Have I received Communion in a state of mortal sin?

4th Commandment — Honor your father and your mother.

  • Have I disrespected my parents, in word, tone, or action?
  • Have I neglected aging parents who need my time or care?
  • Have I failed to discipline, instruct, or be present to my own children?
  • Have I disobeyed lawful authority — civil, ecclesiastical, employer — through pride or laziness?

5th Commandment — You shall not kill.

  • Have I held anger, hatred, or grudges against another person?
  • Have I used contraception or participated in an abortion in any way?
  • Have I harmed my own body through excess drinking, drug use, eating disorders, or reckless behavior?
  • Have I been violent in word — verbal cruelty, sarcasm meant to wound, public humiliation?
  • Have I refused to forgive someone who has asked, or refused to seek forgiveness from someone I have wronged?
  • Have I given scandal — led others into sin by my example?

6th and 9th Commandments — You shall not commit adultery / covet your neighbor's spouse.

  • Have I engaged in sexual activity outside marriage?
  • Have I committed adultery, including emotional adultery?
  • Have I used pornography, masturbated, or entertained impure thoughts willingly?
  • Have I dressed, spoken, or behaved in ways meant to provoke lust in others?
  • Have I been unfaithful to my spouse in attention, energy, or emotional intimacy with another person?

7th and 10th Commandments — You shall not steal / covet your neighbor's goods.

  • Have I taken what does not belong to me — including small office supplies, time at work I was paid for and did not give, software or media I did not pay for?
  • Have I cheated on taxes, on tests, on expense reports?
  • Have I failed to pay debts I could have paid?
  • Have I envied another's success, possessions, or relationships?
  • Have I been greedy — making money or accumulation the goal of my life?
  • Have I given to the poor in proportion to what I have been given?

8th Commandment — You shall not bear false witness.

  • Have I lied — including the lies of exaggeration, omission, and silence?
  • Have I gossiped, spread rumors, or repeated something I should not have?
  • Have I damaged another person's reputation, even with true facts unnecessarily shared?
  • Have I broken a confidence?
  • Have I judged others in my heart based on incomplete knowledge?

II. Examination by the Seven Capital Sins

Pride

  • Have I taken credit for what was a gift?
  • Have I refused to listen, refused to apologize, refused to ask for help?
  • Have I been more concerned with appearing right than being right?
  • Have I despised others mentally — for their politics, their accent, their tastes, their bodies?

Envy

  • Have I been sorrowful at another's good fortune?
  • Have I quietly hoped someone else would fail?
  • Have I let comparison steal my gratitude?

Wrath

  • Have I exploded in anger when patience was called for?
  • Have I nursed resentment, replaying conversations and grievances?
  • Have I taken out frustration on people who did not deserve it — family, employees, the cashier?

Sloth

  • Have I avoided prayer because it was hard?
  • Have I avoided work I committed to do?
  • Have I let entertainment, scrolling, or numbing take the place of real engagement with my life?
  • Have I refused difficult duties — to my family, my work, my soul — because they were uncomfortable?

Gluttony

  • Have I overeaten or overdrunk to numbness or excess?
  • Have I made food, drink, or comfort an idol?
  • Have I broken fasts of obligation through indifference?

Greed

  • Have I treated wealth as security instead of as a stewardship?
  • Have I withheld from those who needed what I had?
  • Have I let possessions or status drive decisions that should have been driven by love?

Lust

  • Have I treated another person as an object for my use, in fantasy or in action?
  • Have I tolerated what should not be tolerated in my media, my conversation, my thoughts?
  • Have I betrayed a relationship by attention given elsewhere?

III. Sins of Omission

These are the easiest to miss because they leave no trace. The good you did not do.

  • The person you knew needed a call you did not make
  • The wrong you witnessed and did not name
  • The opportunity to help that you let pass because helping was inconvenient
  • The talents, time, or resources you have not used for any purpose worthy of them
  • The forgiveness you have not extended
  • The truth you have not spoken

IV. How to Name Them

For each sin, the formula is simple. Kind (what type of sin) and, for grave matter, number (how many times since your last confession). Examples:

  • "I missed Mass on Sunday twice through my own fault."
  • "I used pornography on several occasions."
  • "I lied to my spouse about money."
  • "I held anger against my mother and refused to speak to her for two months."
  • "I gossiped about a coworker in a way that damaged his reputation."

You do not need to give the priest the story. He does not need the context. Name the sin, name the kind, give a rough number for grave matter, and let the rest go. For the full mechanics of the sacrament, see our guides on how to go to confession and the act of contrition.

Final encouragement: every sin on this list has been confessed before, by someone, somewhere, in worse forms. There is nothing you can bring to the confessional that has not been brought already. Bring what is yours, name it as best you can, and accept the absolution. The doors open in.
