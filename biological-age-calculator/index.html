Biological Age Calculator: The Major Tests Compared

PhenoAge, GrimAge, DunedinPACE, Horvath — what they measure, what they cost, which one to choose.

A biological age calculator estimates how much wear, damage, and decline your body has accumulated, expressed as a number you can compare to your chronological age. The major tests differ in what they measure, how accurate they are, and how much they cost. This guide walks through the four most-validated calculators side by side, names which one to pick for which purpose, and points to a free option you can calculate from a standard blood panel.

The Four Major Calculators

PhenoAge

Cost: Free if you have a recent CBC and metabolic panel · Available online and through several commercial labs

Developed by Morgan Levine in 2018. PhenoAge calculates biological age from nine standard clinical biomarkers: albumin, creatinine, glucose, log C-reactive protein, lymphocyte percent, mean cell volume, red cell distribution width, alkaline phosphatase, and white blood cell count. All nine appear on a routine annual physical. If you have recent labs, you can calculate your PhenoAge right now using the published formula or any of the free online tools.

PhenoAge is the most accessible test on the list and the right starting point for most readers. It predicts mortality and morbidity better than chronological age. Its weakness is that it can be slow to reflect intervention effects.

Horvath Clock

Cost: $200-300 · Available through TruDiagnostic, Elysium, and several research labs

The original epigenetic clock, published by Steve Horvath at UCLA in 2013. Measures DNA methylation at 353 specific sites and outputs an age estimate that works across tissues (blood, saliva, even some tissue biopsies). The original Horvath clock correlates well with chronological age but is a relatively weaker predictor of mortality and disease compared to later clocks.

The Horvath clock is most useful in research contexts. For an individual deciding whether interventions are working, the newer mortality-trained clocks (PhenoAge, GrimAge) and the rate-of-aging clock (DunedinPACE) are usually more informative.

GrimAge and GrimAge2

Cost: $300-500 · Available through TruDiagnostic and a small number of other commercial labs

Developed by Lu et al. at UCLA in 2019. GrimAge is currently the most predictive of all-cause mortality of any widely-available test. It is trained on DNA methylation patterns plus plasma proteins linked to lifespan, including markers of inflammation and smoking exposure (the "Grim" in the name refers to mortality risk).

GrimAge is the gold standard for individuals who want a single number that best estimates "how fast you are biologically aging compared to your peers." It is expensive but it is currently the best money can buy for this purpose.

DunedinPACE

Cost: $300-500 · Available through TruDiagnostic and several research-affiliated labs

Developed by the Dunedin Multidisciplinary Health and Development Study in 2022. DunedinPACE measures something different from the other tests: not biological age at a moment but the rate at which you are aging right now (pace of aging per chronological year). A score of 1.0 means you are aging at the average rate. Below 1.0 is slower; above 1.0 is faster.

DunedinPACE is particularly responsive to intervention. If you want to know whether your sleep, training, or supplement changes are working, this is the most actionable test on the market.

The Free Option

If you have a recent comprehensive blood panel (CBC and basic metabolic panel), you can calculate your PhenoAge right now without paying for a specialty test. Three free online calculators implement the published formula:

  • PhenoAge.com — the simplest interface
  • BioAgeCalc.com — adds historical tracking
  • Levine Lab's published spreadsheet — the most academically rigorous source

If you have any results from a Quest or LabCorp panel in the last twelve months, plug them in and you have a free baseline. This is the right starting point for anyone curious about the field without the budget for a commercial test.

How Often to Test

Annually is the right cadence for most calculators. The clocks move slowly, and quarterly testing is mostly noise. The one exception is DunedinPACE — because it measures rate rather than state, some readers benefit from twice-yearly testing during an active intervention period to detect whether changes are working.

The trap to avoid: chasing the score. The number is a proxy for what matters (long, healthy life), not the thing itself. Optimize for how you feel and function. Use the calculator to check that the optimization is producing measurable underlying change.
