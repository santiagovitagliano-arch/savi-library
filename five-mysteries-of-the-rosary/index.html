The 5 Mysteries of the Rosary: Joyful, Sorrowful, Glorious, Luminous

All four sets, the Scripture, the meditation, and the day-of-week schedule.

The Holy Rosary contains four sets of five mysteries — twenty meditations in total — drawn from the central events of the life of Jesus Christ. Each set is prayed on specific days, and each mystery offers a particular grace to those who pray it with attention. This guide walks through all twenty, with the Scripture reference, the meditation, and the traditional fruit of each.

I. The Schedule by Day

  • Monday and Saturday — The Joyful Mysteries
  • Tuesday and Friday — The Sorrowful Mysteries
  • Wednesday and Sunday — The Glorious Mysteries
  • Thursday — The Luminous Mysteries

The Luminous Mysteries were added by Pope John Paul II in his 2002 apostolic letter Rosarium Virginis Mariae. The traditional rosary before 2002 contained only the Joyful, Sorrowful, and Glorious sets.

For the step-by-step prayer instructions, see our companion guide on how to pray the rosary.

II. The Joyful Mysteries (Monday & Saturday)

1. The Annunciation

The angel Gabriel announces to Mary that she will conceive and bear the Son of God. Her fiat — "let it be done unto me according to thy word" — is the act of consent on which the rest of salvation history hinges.

Luke 1:26–38 · Fruit: Humility

2. The Visitation

Mary travels to visit her cousin Elizabeth, who is pregnant with John the Baptist. The unborn John leaps in his mother's womb at Mary's greeting. Elizabeth speaks the words "blessed art thou among women."

Luke 1:39–56 · Fruit: Love of neighbor

3. The Nativity

Jesus is born in Bethlehem, laid in a manger, and worshipped by shepherds. The infinite enters the finite. God becomes small enough to be held.

Luke 2:1–20 · Fruit: Poverty of spirit

4. The Presentation in the Temple

Mary and Joseph bring the infant Jesus to the Temple. Simeon recognizes him as the Christ and prophesies that a sword will pierce Mary's heart.

Luke 2:22–38 · Fruit: Obedience and purity

5. The Finding of Jesus in the Temple

Jesus, at twelve, is lost for three days and is found in the Temple discussing with the teachers. "Did you not know that I must be about my Father's business?"

Luke 2:41–52 · Fruit: Piety, seeking Jesus

III. The Sorrowful Mysteries (Tuesday & Friday)

1. The Agony in the Garden

Jesus prays in Gethsemane the night before his crucifixion. He sweats blood. He asks that the cup pass from him — and then accepts the Father's will.

Matthew 26:36–46 · Fruit: Sorrow for sin

2. The Scourging at the Pillar

Jesus is bound and scourged by the Roman soldiers. The physical violence visited on the body that contained the Word.

John 19:1 · Fruit: Purity of body

3. The Crowning with Thorns

The soldiers mock Jesus as a false king, crowning him with a circle of thorns and dressing him in purple. The mockery is itself a prophecy they do not understand.

Matthew 27:27–31 · Fruit: Moral courage

4. The Carrying of the Cross

Jesus carries the cross to Calvary, falls three times, meets his mother along the way, and is helped by Simon of Cyrene.

Luke 23:26–32 · Fruit: Patience under suffering

5. The Crucifixion

Jesus is nailed to the cross, suffers three hours, and dies. From the cross he commits Mary to John and John to Mary. He says: "It is finished."

John 19:17–37 · Fruit: Self-denial, final perseverance

IV. The Glorious Mysteries (Wednesday & Sunday)

1. The Resurrection

On the third day Jesus rises from the dead. The tomb is empty. Death is defeated. The central event of the Christian faith — without which the rest is in vain.

Matthew 28:1–10 · Fruit: Faith

2. The Ascension

Forty days after the Resurrection, Jesus ascends into heaven, promising to send the Holy Spirit. The work continues now through the body of believers.

Acts 1:6–11 · Fruit: Hope and longing for heaven

3. The Descent of the Holy Spirit

At Pentecost, the Holy Spirit descends on Mary and the apostles. Tongues of fire. The first proclamation. The birth of the body of believers as a public reality.

Acts 2:1–13 · Fruit: Love of God, gifts of the Spirit

4. The Assumption of Mary

Mary, at the end of her earthly life, is taken up body and soul into heaven. The first fulfillment of the resurrection promise for any human creature other than Christ himself.

Defined dogma 1950 · Fruit: Grace of a holy death

5. The Coronation of Mary

Mary is crowned Queen of Heaven and Earth. The mother of the Son becomes the mother of every Christian.

Revelation 12:1 · Fruit: Devotion to Mary, trust in her intercession

V. The Luminous Mysteries (Thursday)

1. The Baptism of Jesus in the Jordan

John the Baptist baptizes Jesus. The heavens open. The Father's voice declares: "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased."

Matthew 3:13–17 · Fruit: Openness to the Holy Spirit

2. The Wedding at Cana

At Mary's request, Jesus performs his first public miracle — turning water into wine. The note to remember: "Do whatever he tells you."

John 2:1–12 · Fruit: To Jesus through Mary

3. The Proclamation of the Kingdom

Jesus preaches the Kingdom of God, calls people to repentance, forgives sins. The whole of his public ministry in one meditation.

Mark 1:14–15 · Fruit: Repentance and trust

4. The Transfiguration

On Mount Tabor, Jesus is transfigured before Peter, James, and John. His face shines. His clothing becomes brilliant white. The voice from the cloud: "Listen to him."

Matthew 17:1–8 · Fruit: Desire for holiness

5. The Institution of the Eucharist

At the Last Supper, Jesus takes bread and wine and gives himself: "This is my body... This is my blood." The sacrament that remains the center of Catholic life.

Luke 22:14–20 · Fruit: Eucharistic devotion

VI. A Note on Meditation

The mysteries are not items to check off. They are doorways. Each time you announce a mystery during a rosary, you are not reminding yourself of an event — you are entering a scene to be present to it. Some days the Annunciation will be Mary's quiet courage; other days it will be the angel's strange presence; other days it will be the unmade choice still hanging in the room. The same scene reveals different things to a soul that has changed since the last time it was prayed.

This is what distinguishes the rosary from any other form of repetitive prayer in the world. The Hail Marys are the rhythm. The mysteries are the music. The music can vary infinitely without the rhythm ever needing to change.
