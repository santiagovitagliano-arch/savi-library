Metabolic Age Calculator: What It Is and How to Lower It

The number on the smart scale, what it actually means, and the three levers that move it.

If you have ever stepped on a body-composition scale and seen a "metabolic age" that did not match your driver's license, you are not alone. The number is real, the methodology is straightforward, and the levers that lower it are within reach. This guide explains exactly how metabolic age is calculated, how it differs from biological age, and what to actually do about it.

What Metabolic Age Is

Metabolic age is a comparison of your resting metabolic rate (RMR) — the number of calories your body burns at rest — against the average RMR for people of your chronological age. If your RMR matches the average thirty-year-old's RMR while you are fifty chronologically, your metabolic age is thirty. If your RMR matches the average sixty-year-old's RMR while you are forty chronologically, your metabolic age is sixty.

The metric is a proxy for one specific thing: how much metabolically active tissue (mostly muscle) you have for your weight. A higher RMR almost always means more muscle. More muscle almost always means better metabolic health, better glucose regulation, better strength, better function, lower mortality risk.

How Metabolic Age Differs From Biological Age

This is the most common confusion in the field, so it is worth being clear.

  • Metabolic age is calculated from body composition and metabolic rate. It is fast, cheap, and reflects mostly muscle mass and lean tissue.
  • Biological age is calculated from cellular markers (DNA methylation, blood biomarkers, protein levels). It is more comprehensive, more expensive, and reflects cumulative cellular damage across many systems.

You can have a great metabolic age (lots of muscle) and a poor biological age (high inflammation, smoking history, sleep deprivation). You can have a great biological age and a mediocre metabolic age (lean but undermuscled). The two metrics complement each other. For the biological age side, see our companion article on biological age.

How to Calculate Metabolic Age

The standard calculation method has two steps.

Step 1: Calculate your RMR

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is the modern standard:

Men: RMR = (10 × weight kg) + (6.25 × height cm) − (5 × age yrs) + 5
Women: RMR = (10 × weight kg) + (6.25 × height cm) − (5 × age yrs) − 161

This gives an estimated RMR based on chronological age. The Katch-McArdle equation is more accurate if you know your body fat percentage, because it scales RMR to lean body mass directly:

Both sexes: RMR = 370 + (21.6 × lean body mass kg)
where lean body mass = total weight − (weight × body fat %)

Step 2: Compare to age-average RMR tables

Your metabolic age is the chronological age whose average RMR most closely matches yours. Smart scales (Tanita, Withings, Renpho) compute this automatically using their internal tables and bioimpedance measurement of body composition.

The Easy Path

You do not need to do the math by hand. Any modern body-composition scale gives you a metabolic age estimate in 30 seconds. The accuracy of smart-scale metabolic age estimates is roughly ±3 to ±5 years — useful for tracking trends, less useful as an absolute number. The trend matters more than the single reading.

The Three Levers That Lower Metabolic Age

Lever 1: Build Muscle

The single most powerful intervention. Resistance training two to four hours per week — heavy enough to challenge, progressive — adds metabolically active tissue. More muscle means higher RMR means lower metabolic age. This lever moves the number more than any other.

The mistake most readers make: trying to lower metabolic age through cardio alone. Cardio improves cardiovascular fitness (which matters enormously for biological age) but does not build the muscle that metabolic age is measuring. Both forms of training matter; they do different jobs.

Lever 2: Lose Excess Fat (Without Losing Muscle)

Excess body fat lowers the ratio of metabolically active tissue to total weight. A slow, sustainable cut — small caloric deficit, adequate protein (1.2-1.6g/kg body weight), continued resistance training — drops fat while preserving muscle. Aggressive crash diets do the opposite: they preserve fat and burn muscle, which raises metabolic age.

Lever 3: Sleep and Recovery

Chronic sleep deprivation degrades insulin sensitivity, increases cortisol, accelerates muscle loss, and impairs the recovery that lets resistance training produce gains. Seven to nine hours of consistent sleep is the foundation that makes the first two levers work.

What Will Not Lower Your Metabolic Age

  • Most supplements. Metabolic age is driven by tissue composition, not by molecular interventions. A green tea extract will not move it. (For the longer evaluation of supplements, see longevity supplements.)
  • Pure cardio. Excellent for the heart, neutral for metabolic age. Both matter.
  • Extreme caloric restriction. Burns muscle along with fat. Raises metabolic age.
  • Bioidentical hormone optimization without the foundational lifestyle work. Sometimes useful as a complement; useless as a substitute.

How Often to Check

Monthly is reasonable if you have a smart scale. Quarterly is the better cadence — body composition does not change fast, and weekly readings are mostly noise from hydration. Watch the trend over a year, not the variance over a week.

A typical adult who implements the three levers consistently can lower metabolic age by 5-15 years over 12-24 months. The improvements plateau eventually, but the plateau is several years below where most adults start.

The Bigger Picture

Metabolic age is a useful proxy for one part of physical aging. It is not the whole story. The full story includes cardiovascular fitness (VO2 max), biological age (cellular markers), and — equally important — the inner-life inputs the longevity field is finally taking seriously: purpose, community, faith.
