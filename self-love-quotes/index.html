Self Love Quotes: 60 Lines from the Saints, Poets, and Philosophers

A curated collection treating self-love as a virtue rather than a feeling.

The phrase self love has been borrowed by an entire industry of greeting cards, mirror affirmations, and pastel infographics. Almost none of it would survive a serious reading of the writers who took the subject most carefully. This collection gathers sixty lines on self-love from the saints, the mystics, the poets, and the philosophers — writers who treated the love of oneself as a moral question and an arduous practice, not a mood.

I. The Old Distinction Worth Reclaiming

Before the quotations, the distinction. The Christian tradition has always made room for a true self-love and warned about a false one. The true love of self is the love that orders the self toward what is good for it — toward virtue, integrity, the slow becoming of the person one was made to be. The false love of self is what the older writers called amor sui in the unhealthy sense: self-preference, vanity, the worship of one's preferences as if they were laws.

Aquinas wrote that the second great commandment — love your neighbor as yourself — presumes that you already love yourself rightly. If you do not love yourself rightly, you cannot love your neighbor rightly either. Self-love and neighbor-love are the same act under different names.

The quotations that follow come from writers who understood that. Read slowly. Many of these lines reward returning.

II. From the Saints and Mystics

"To fall in love with God is the greatest of all romances; to seek Him, the greatest adventure; to find Him, the greatest human achievement."— Saint Augustine of Hippo
"You yourself, as much as anybody in the entire universe, deserve your love and affection. — Until a man finds himself, he is at home nowhere."— Often attributed to the Buddha; popularized in modern translation
"Be gentle with yourself. You are a child of the universe, no less than the trees and the stars."— Max Ehrmann, Desiderata
"How you love yourself is how you teach others to love you."— Rupi Kaur
"Let nothing disturb you. Let nothing frighten you. All things are passing. God alone is unchanging."— Saint Teresa of Ávila
"We are the ones we have been waiting for."— Hopi Elders, attributed; later June Jordan
"Almost everything will work again if you unplug it for a few minutes, including you."— Anne Lamott
"You are imperfect, permanently and inevitably flawed. And you are beautiful."— Amy Bloom
"To love oneself is the beginning of a lifelong romance."— Oscar Wilde, An Ideal Husband
"The privilege of a lifetime is to become who you truly are."— Carl Jung

III. From C.S. Lewis

Lewis wrote against the cheap version of self-love more than he wrote for the real version, which is part of why his lines on the subject land with such force. The line below is one of the clearest things ever written about the proper place of pride and humility.

"Humility is not thinking less of yourself; it is thinking of yourself less."— C.S. Lewis, Mere Christianity
"You don't have a soul. You are a soul. You have a body."— Often attributed to C.S. Lewis (the exact wording is George MacDonald-adjacent)
"There are no ordinary people. You have never talked to a mere mortal."— C.S. Lewis, The Weight of Glory
"A man's reach should exceed his grasp, or what's a heaven for?"— Robert Browning

IV. From the Contemplatives

"There is a hard law. When an injury is done to us, we never recover until we forgive."— Alan Paton
"The thing that is really hard, and really amazing, is giving up on being perfect and beginning the work of becoming yourself."— Anna Quindlen
"You yourself, as much as anybody in the entire universe, deserve your love and affection."— Sharon Salzberg
"For a seed to achieve its greatest expression, it must come completely undone. The shell cracks, its insides come out, and everything changes. To someone who doesn't understand growth, it would look like complete destruction."— Cynthia Occelli
"What you seek is seeking you."— Rumi
"The wound is the place where the Light enters you."— Rumi
"Do not seek the truth, only cease to cherish opinions."— Seng-ts'an, third Zen patriarch
"The art of being yourself at your best is the art of unfolding your personality into the person you want to be. — Be gentle. Be honest. Be brave."— Wilfred Peterson
"Each person must live their life as a model for others."— Rosa Parks
"You alone are enough. You have nothing to prove to anybody."— Maya Angelou
"Talk to yourself like you would to someone you love."— Brené Brown
"Tell me, what is it you plan to do with your one wild and precious life?"— Mary Oliver

V. From the Poets

"Be patient toward all that is unsolved in your heart, and try to love the questions themselves."— Rainer Maria Rilke, Letters to a Young Poet
"The day came when the risk to remain tight in a bud was more painful than the risk it took to blossom."— Anaïs Nin
"And now that you don't have to be perfect, you can be good."— John Steinbeck, East of Eden
"You can search throughout the entire universe for someone who is more deserving of your love and affection than you are yourself, and that person is not to be found anywhere. You yourself deserve your love and affection."— Often attributed to the Buddha (Dhammapada)
"Owning our story can be hard, but not nearly as difficult as spending our lives running from it."— Brené Brown
"Lighten up on yourself. No one is perfect. Gently accept your humanness."— Deborah Day
"To be beautiful means to be yourself. You don't need to be accepted by others. You need to accept yourself."— Thich Nhat Hanh
"And I said to my body, softly, 'I want to be your friend.' It took a long breath and replied, 'I have been waiting my whole life for this.'"— Nayyirah Waheed

VI. The Hard Ones

The lines below cut against the sentimental version of self-love. They belong in the same collection precisely because they keep the soft lines honest.

"It is not the critic who counts; not the man who points out how the strong man stumbles. The credit belongs to the man who is actually in the arena."— Theodore Roosevelt
"Between stimulus and response there is a space. In that space is our power to choose our response. In our response lies our growth and our freedom."— Viktor Frankl
"Discipline is the highest form of self-love."— Ancient saying, attributed widely
"The most terrifying thing is to accept oneself completely."— Carl Jung
"Self-pity is easily the most destructive of the non-pharmaceutical narcotics; it is addictive, gives momentary pleasure and separates the victim from reality."— John Gardner
"You have power over your mind — not outside events. Realize this, and you will find strength."— Marcus Aurelius, Meditations
"Pity me not the long descent. The down-slope is a learning. The valley a teacher."— Modern proverb
"It is not your love that sustains the marriage, but from now on, the marriage that sustains your love."— Dietrich Bonhoeffer (on commitment, but the principle holds for the self)
"As I began to love myself I freed myself of anything that is no good for my health — food, people, things, situations, and everything that drew me down and away from myself."— Charlie Chaplin
"To love is to be vulnerable."— C.S. Lewis, The Four Loves
"The privilege of being yourself is the only privilege worth fighting for."— Joseph Campbell, paraphrased
"Almost everything you do will seem insignificant, but it is very important that you do it."— Mahatma Gandhi
"Be who you are and say what you feel, because those who mind don't matter and those who matter don't mind."— Bernard Baruch
"Don't ask what the world needs. Ask what makes you come alive, and go do it. Because what the world needs is people who have come alive."— Howard Thurman
"Stop acting so small. You are the universe in ecstatic motion."— Rumi
"He who has a why to live for can bear almost any how."— Friedrich Nietzsche, cited by Viktor Frankl
"Love yourself. Forgive yourself. Be true to yourself. How you treat yourself sets the standard for how others will treat you."— Steve Maraboli
"The most difficult phase of life is not when no one understands you. It is when you do not understand yourself."— Unknown, often attributed to Kahlil Gibran
"You yourself deserve your love and affection. — And so does the person beside you."— Adapted from Sharon Salzberg
"Be the person you needed when you were younger."— Ayesha Siddiqi
"It is never too late to be what you might have been."— George Eliot
"What lies behind us and what lies before us are tiny matters compared to what lies within us."— Ralph Waldo Emerson
"The privilege of a lifetime is being who you are."— Joseph Campbell
"You were born with wings. Why prefer to crawl through life?"— Rumi
"At any given moment, you have the power to say: this is not how the story is going to end."— Christine Mason Miller
"Love is not affectionate feeling, but a steady wish for the loved person's ultimate good as far as it can be obtained."— C.S. Lewis

— And this last one applies to the self most of all.
