Spiritual Awakening Symptoms: The 10 Physical and Emotional Signs

What the mystical tradition recognizes — and what to do about it.

Most lists of "10 physical symptoms of spiritual awakening" range from useful to absurd. The Christian mystical tradition has been describing this territory carefully for two thousand years. This guide gives you the ten genuine physical and emotional symptoms — drawn from the saints and mystics, not from wellness blogs — along with what they actually mean and what to do when you recognize them in yourself.

What a Spiritual Awakening Is

Before the list, the framing. A spiritual awakening is a slow re-ordering of attention. The center of gravity of a life shifts from the surface of things to what lies beneath them. This shift produces real, measurable changes in the body and the emotions. The tradition takes these changes seriously. For the full framing — the three classical stages, the dark night, the practical path — see our companion guide on spiritual awakening.

The 10 Symptoms

1 Sleep Disturbances

The most commonly reported symptom. Both insomnia and unusually vivid, often spiritually-charged dreams. The interior life intensifies; the unconscious works harder. The tradition treats this as normal during periods of inner work and counsels patience rather than medication unless the disruption becomes clinically severe.

2 Changes in Appetite

Both directions are common. Eating less is more typical in the early stages. Some readers report a spontaneous loss of interest in foods or substances they previously craved (alcohol, processed foods, sweets). The body responds to interior shifts in ways that have not been fully mapped.

3 Sensitivity to Noise, Crowds, and Screens

What previously did not register now exhausts. Loud bars, busy malls, hours of social-media scrolling, news cycles — all become disproportionately taxing. This is the nervous system recalibrating to a quieter internal baseline. The healthy response is to reduce exposure, not to medicate the sensitivity away.

4 Tears at Unexpected Moments

Tears during prayer, music, conversation, sunsets. The tradition calls this the gift of tears and treats it with reverence. It is not depression. It is a softening of the inner life. Many saints prayed for the gift; many received it.

5 Loss of Interest in Things That Previously Consumed You

Entertainments, status games, certain friendships, certain ambitions. The pull weakens. This can feel like loss before it feels like freedom. The tradition counsels not forcing the change but also not resisting it — let the interests that no longer serve you fall away naturally.

6 Increased Interest in Things You Previously Dismissed

Silence. Walking. Reading. Prayer. Ordinary work. The company of children. Older books. Older music. The mystics noted that these "older" interests are not regressions but recoveries — return to what was always there underneath the noise.

7 Emotional Volatility

Particularly in early purgative stages. Old wounds surface for processing. The defenses that previously kept memories or feelings buried weaken. This phase is uncomfortable but productive, like a fever in a body fighting infection. It typically settles within months.

8 Synchronicity

A heightened awareness of patterns and meaningful coincidences. Words from a sermon land on a sentence you just thought. A book opens to exactly the passage you needed. A stranger says something that answers a question you have not spoken aloud. The tradition is cautious here — this is real but easily over-interpreted. Treat it as a pat on the shoulder, not a personal directive from the universe.

9 Energy Shifts

Periods of unusual stamina alternating with periods of deep fatigue. Some days you can do twice your normal work; some days you cannot get out of a chair. The tradition counsels learning to follow these rhythms rather than overriding them.

10 A Felt Sense of Being Watched, Accompanied, or Held

Often subtle. Never grandiose. If it is grandiose, it is probably not the genuine article. The classical description is a quiet sense that you are not alone in your own interior — that someone friendly is present in the room of yourself. The tradition treats this as a real perception of the indwelling Presence that has always been there but that the cluttered mind could not detect.

What Is Not a Symptom

The internet adds many things to lists like this that the tradition would not endorse. A short list of what is not a spiritual awakening symptom.

  • Sudden grandiosity. The belief that you are uniquely chosen, uniquely gifted, or one of the "chosen ones" is the opposite of a real awakening, which produces humility rather than self-importance.
  • An urge to teach before you have learned. Genuine awakening produces quietness, not lectures.
  • Contempt for those who have not had your experience. Real awakening sees others more clearly and more compassionately, not less.
  • Severe physical symptoms — chronic pain, neurological changes, weight loss without cause. These are medical issues. See a physician first; consider the spiritual dimension second.

What to Do When You Recognize the Symptoms

  1. Do not panic. The symptoms above are well-documented and well-understood. You are not unusual.
  2. Do not try to manufacture more. Trying to produce spiritual experiences is the surest way to abort the real ones.
  3. Establish a daily silence. Twenty minutes a day. Same time, same place. The single most important thing you can do.
  4. Find a guide. A spiritual director, an honest pastor, a confessor. The inner work goes much better with one experienced companion. (See our guide on how to go to confession.)
  5. Read slowly. One book at a time. The classics. (See spiritual awakening books for the curated list.)
  6. Be patient. Most of these symptoms move through phases over months and years. Resist the urge to declare yourself "done" with any of them. They come back, in different forms, at different depths.
