How to Pray the Rosary: A Step-by-Step Guide

All prayers, all four mysteries, the daily schedule, and how to pray it every day.

The rosary is the central Marian devotion of the Catholic tradition and the prayer most often picked up by returning Catholics. This guide gives you the step-by-step practice, all the prayers in the order they are prayed, the four sets of mysteries with their day-of-week schedule, and the small practical adjustments that make praying it every day possible.

I. What the Rosary Is

The rosary is a meditative prayer on the central events of the life of Jesus Christ, prayed through the eyes of His mother. The beads are a counting tool, not a magical object. They free the hands and the mind so attention can rest on the mystery being meditated, rather than on keeping track of where you are. The full rosary is twenty mysteries; on any given day, you pray five of them. A complete five-decade rosary takes about twenty minutes of unhurried praying.

The structure is built from a small set of repeating prayers and a large set of contemplative material. The mind has the prayers to anchor it; the heart has the mysteries to dwell in. The repetition is the point — it is what allows attention to deepen rather than scatter.

II. The Prayers You Need to Know

Six prayers form the entire vocabulary of the rosary. If you can pray these six, you can pray the rosary.

The Apostles' Creed

Prayed Once at the Beginning
I believe in God, the Father almighty, Creator of heaven and earth, and in Jesus Christ, His only Son, our Lord, who was conceived by the Holy Spirit, born of the Virgin Mary, suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died, and was buried; He descended into hell; on the third day He rose again from the dead; He ascended into heaven, and is seated at the right hand of God the Father almighty; from there He will come to judge the living and the dead. I believe in the Holy Spirit, the holy catholic Church, the communion of saints, the forgiveness of sins, the resurrection of the body, and life everlasting. Amen.

The Our Father (Pater Noster)

Prayed Six Times Per Rosary
Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be Thy name; Thy kingdom come, Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread, and forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against us; and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Amen.

The Hail Mary (Ave Maria)

Prayed Fifty-Three Times Per Rosary
Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

The Glory Be (Doxology)

Prayed Six Times Per Rosary
Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.

The Fatima Prayer (Optional)

Prayed After Each Glory Be
O my Jesus, forgive us our sins, save us from the fires of hell, and lead all souls to heaven, especially those in most need of Thy mercy. Amen.

The Hail Holy Queen (Salve Regina)

Prayed Once at the End
Hail, holy Queen, mother of mercy, our life, our sweetness, and our hope. To thee do we cry, poor banished children of Eve. To thee do we send up our sighs, mourning and weeping in this valley of tears. Turn then, most gracious advocate, thine eyes of mercy toward us, and after this our exile, show unto us the blessed fruit of thy womb, Jesus. O clement, O loving, O sweet Virgin Mary. Pray for us, O holy Mother of God, that we may be made worthy of the promises of Christ. Amen.

III. Step by Step (for Beginners)

Hold the rosary with the crucifix in your hand. Then follow this order. Each step is timed to a bead.

  1. On the crucifix — Make the Sign of the Cross. Pray the Apostles' Creed.
  2. On the first large bead — Pray one Our Father.
  3. On the next three small beads — Pray three Hail Marys (traditionally for an increase of faith, hope, and charity).
  4. On the space before the next large bead — Pray one Glory Be.
  5. Announce the first mystery of the day (see the table below). On the next large bead, pray one Our Father while meditating on the mystery.
  6. On the next ten small beads (one decade) — Pray ten Hail Marys, continuing to meditate on the mystery.
  7. At the end of the decade — Pray one Glory Be. Then, if you wish, the Fatima Prayer.
  8. Announce the second mystery, then repeat steps 5–7. Repeat again for the third, fourth, and fifth mysteries.
  9. After the fifth decade — Pray the Hail Holy Queen. Close with the Sign of the Cross.

That is the entire rosary. The total prayer count is fifty-three Hail Marys, six Our Fathers, six Glory Bes, one Creed, and one Hail Holy Queen — anchored by five meditations.

IV. The Four Sets of Mysteries (and the Day Schedule)

There are twenty mysteries in total, grouped into four sets of five. Each set is traditionally prayed on specific days.

DayMysteries
Monday and SaturdayJoyful Mysteries
Tuesday and FridaySorrowful Mysteries
Wednesday and SundayGlorious Mysteries
ThursdayLuminous Mysteries

Each set contains five specific scenes. We treat them at full depth in the companion guide on the 5 mysteries of the rosary. The brief version:

The Joyful Mysteries

  1. The Annunciation
  2. The Visitation
  3. The Nativity
  4. The Presentation in the Temple
  5. The Finding of Jesus in the Temple

The Sorrowful Mysteries

  1. The Agony in the Garden
  2. The Scourging at the Pillar
  3. The Crowning with Thorns
  4. The Carrying of the Cross
  5. The Crucifixion

The Glorious Mysteries

  1. The Resurrection
  2. The Ascension
  3. The Descent of the Holy Spirit
  4. The Assumption of Mary
  5. The Coronation of Mary

The Luminous Mysteries

  1. The Baptism of Jesus in the Jordan
  2. The Wedding at Cana
  3. The Proclamation of the Kingdom
  4. The Transfiguration
  5. The Institution of the Eucharist

The Luminous Mysteries were added by Pope John Paul II in 2002. They cover the public ministry of Christ, which the original three sets did not address directly.

V. How to Pray the Rosary Every Day

The single best advice from anyone who prays the rosary daily is the same: choose a fixed time and a fixed place, then guard them. The rosary that has to fit into a schedule rarely gets prayed. The rosary that is the schedule almost always does.

Common choices: first thing in the morning before coffee, during a daily walk, on the commute (if you drive), or as the last thing before sleep. Twenty minutes is the ordinary length. If twenty is impossible, pray one decade. One decade per day will form a habit faster than five decades attempted and abandoned.

If your attention wanders during a decade, do not start the decade over. The wandering is normal and unavoidable. Return to the bead you are on and continue. Distraction is not failure; staying with the rosary through distraction is the prayer.

VI. A Few Honest Notes for Returning Catholics

If you have not prayed the rosary in years, you will feel the beads as awkward and the prayers as mechanical for the first week or two. This is universal. The fingers learn the beads; the mind learns to hold the mysteries. By the end of the second week, the rosary begins to pray you instead of you praying it.

Do not worry about whether you are "doing it right." There is no advanced or beginner rosary. The same prayer is prayed by the cardinal and the child. The differences in depth come from a lifetime of praying it, not from technique.

If the Hail Mary feels strange because you have not addressed Mary directly before, sit with the feeling. The Catholic understanding is that Mary intercedes — she carries our prayers to her Son the way a mother carries a child's request to a father. Praying with her is not praying instead of Christ; it is praying alongside the first and best disciple.

VII. The Closing Prayers

After the Hail Holy Queen, many people add a short concluding prayer.

Concluding Prayer (Traditional)
O God, whose Only Begotten Son, by His life, death, and resurrection, has purchased for us the rewards of eternal life: grant, we beseech Thee, that meditating upon these mysteries of the Most Holy Rosary of the Blessed Virgin Mary, we may imitate what they contain and obtain what they promise. Through the same Christ our Lord. Amen.

Some also add a prayer for the intentions of the Pope, three Hail Marys for purity, or a personal intention. None of this is required. The rosary as printed above is complete.
